## Qualified clarifications

- The lecture calls Weber a flux-density unit; **Weber measures total flux**, while **Tesla and Gauss measure flux density**. The relation on Lecture 1 slide 28 is still given accurately: 1 T = 1 Wb/m² = 10,000 G.
- The lecture treats orbital and spin magnetic moments in a simplified electron-orbit model. The study notes retain its exam language without implying classical electron orbits are exact quantum mechanics.
- The lecture equates magnetic dipoles with domains. A dipole has N and S poles; a **domain** is a region of aligned magnetic moments.
- The lecture groups common materials as “nonmagnetic”; more precisely, ordinary wood/glass/rubber have negligible magnetic response for classroom purposes.
- Faraday’s law rigorously concerns induced **EMF/voltage**; more current follows at a fixed circuit resistance.
- Lenz’s law says the induced field opposes the **change** in magnetic flux, not necessarily the original field.
- A fixed conductor and a stationary field of **changing strength** can induce voltage without literal physical motion.
- Lecture 2 labels the right-hand grip rule under Fleming’s rule: the conventional **Fleming right-hand rule (generator) and left-hand rule (motor)** are separate from wire/solenoid grip rules.
- Continuous transformer operation requires changing flux (normally AC); steady DC after the initial transient does not give continuous secondary voltage.
- “Inverse square law” is presented as an idealized magnetic-pole force model, not a universal real-world formula for all magnets.
- “Permeability and retentivity inversely proportional” on Lecture 2 slide 2 is a classroom tendency, not a universal mathematical law.
